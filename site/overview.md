# What Nex Order is

Nex Order is order-management software for wholesale distribution: the system a
distributor runs between the moment a customer's order arrives and the moment the
goods leave the dock.

It was built for food and general distribution, where the same business serves
restaurants, hotels and cafés on negotiated pricing, takes orders through several
channels at once, and picks them out of a real warehouse. It is made by NexGen
Innovations in Sydney.

## The problem it addresses

Most distributors are running three systems that disagree with each other: an
inbox where purchase orders arrive as PDFs and email text, a spreadsheet or
accounting package holding prices and stock, and a warehouse whose real contents
are known mainly by the people who work in it.

Nex Order puts those on one ledger. An order can arrive as an email from a
customer's procurement system, be keyed in by an office rep, or be placed by the
customer themselves — and from that point it is the same order, priced the same
way, drawing on the same stock, picked from the same bins.

## What it does

**Orders arrive however the customer sends them.** Purchase orders emailed as
PDFs are read automatically, matched to the right customer and products, and put
in front of an operator to approve. Reps place orders in the field. Customers
order directly against their own tier pricing.

**Pricing is per customer, and it holds.** Tiered pricing, promotions and pack
breaks are resolved in one place, so the price a rep quotes is the price the
invoice carries.

**The warehouse is addressed, not guessed at.** Stock is tracked by product,
location, batch and pallet. Pickers are routed bin by bin along the shortest
walk; goods are put away where the operator's own rules say they belong;
replenishment moves stock to pick faces before they run dry; stocktakes are
counted by location on a handheld.

**Dispatch closes the loop.** Pick slips, dispatch advice, signatures and
invoices come off the same records, so what was picked, what was sent and what
was billed are the same three numbers.

## Who uses it

Six roles, each seeing only its own work: administrators and managers who
configure the catalogue, customers and pricing; field and office sales reps who
place orders and visit customers; warehouse staff who receive, put away, pick and
count; and the distributor's own customers, who see their pricing and their order
history and nothing else.

## How it is delivered

Nex Order is deployed per customer — a distributor gets its own database and its
own instance, not a shared tenancy. Which parts of the product are switched on is
decided per deployment, so a business that wants order management without a
warehouse module does not carry the warehouse, and vice versa. See
[Capabilities](capabilities.md).
